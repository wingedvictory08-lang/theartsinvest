Frequently Asked Questions

studio acquisitions · private sourcing & curation

01

What is Studio Acquisitions?

Studio Acquisitions is the independent curatorial practice of Moment Solomon. I source, acquire, and curate fine art, vintage fashion, and jewelry — and, when the project calls for it, the spaces they belong in.

The practice is based in Chicago and works with clients remotely.

02

What services do you offer?

Private sourcing of fine art, vintage fashion, jewelry, and objects; curatorial consultations; editorial writing; spatial projects; bespoke web design; and, on the real estate side, multifamily co-investment in Chicago.

Each one starts from the same place: knowing what you're searching for, researching the market, and identifying the value of what you find.

03

How do art curation and private sourcing fit under the same practice?

They're two halves of one act. Curating is deciding what belongs — reading a piece, a collection, or a room and understanding why it works. Sourcing is going out and finding it, then acquiring it at the right price.

Taste is a form of self-knowledge and perspective, and acquisition without it is aimless. A curator who can't source has opinions with nowhere to go; a sourcer without a curatorial eye is only shopping. Studio Acquisitions holds both, so every find is researched, placed in context, and chosen for a reason.

04

What can you source for me?

Fine art, vintage fashion, jewelry, watches, furniture, and objects — anything you can describe with intent. Larger briefs can extend to an entire room or property.

Briefs fall into five tiers: a specific find (you know the exact item), brief-based sourcing (you have constraints and an in-person hunt is likely), a curated acquisition (you want an outcome and I choose the piece), collection building (multiple pieces over time), and spatial sourcing (a room or property).

05

How does private sourcing work?

Submit an inquiry on the booking page and select Private Sourcing. You'll receive the intake form, where you describe the piece, your budget ceiling, deadline, measurements, condition tolerance, and dealbreakers. Email the completed form to contactme@studioacquisitions.studio.

I confirm the scope and tier of your brief within 48 hours. Every acquisition is sent to you for approval before purchase, and once approved, the sale is final.

06

How is private sourcing priced?

A non-refundable retainer covers up to 8 hours of active search, and a success fee applies when the brief is filled. The cost of the item itself is paid separately — the retainer and success fee cover the search and delivery, not the object.

If a brief can't be filled within those 8 hours, you can pivot with up to 6 more hours at no charge, extend the search at $40 an hour, or close the brief with the retainer kept.

07

What does a curatorial consultation involve?

A consultation is a focused conversation about taste, style, and acquisition — what you're drawn to, what your collection or space is missing, and where to look for it. It's a good starting point if you're not yet ready for a full sourcing brief.

08

Why does a curatorial practice design websites?

Curation is about how things are seen, and a website is often the first room someone walks into. I build bespoke sites with the same attention to presentation: custom landing pages, multi-page sites, full sites with copy, and investor portals. Packages and current pricing are listed on the booking page.

09

Is the real estate side part of Studio Acquisitions?

Yes. The investment practice applies the same discipline to property: researching under-valued multifamily and mixed-use buildings in Chicago neighborhoods like Wicker Park, West Loop, and Pilsen, underwriting them carefully, and co-investing alongside partners.

10

Where can I read your writing or see your work?

Essays on taste, style, and acquisition are published on the Studio Acquisitions Substack. Weekly artist case studies are on the portfolio site, and a Digital Collection is coming soon.
